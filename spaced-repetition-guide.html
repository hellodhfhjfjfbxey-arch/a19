Chapter 2

Spaced Repetition

Review at the moment you are about to forget, and each memory lasts longer than the last. Here is how to plan it.

1.

The forgetting curve

Without review, much of what we learn fades within a day or two. The decline is steep at first and then levels off. Each well-timed review resets the curve and makes the next decline gentler.

2.

A simple schedule

  1. Learn it today.
  2. Review tomorrow.
  3. Review again in three days.
  4. Then after a week, two weeks and a month.

If you forget something, bring it back to a shorter interval.

3.

Flashcards and boxes

A simple box system works well. Cards you get right move to a box you review less often; cards you get wrong go back to the first box. Paper cards or an app both work, as long as you answer before you look.

4.

What to space

Spacing works for facts, vocabulary, formulas and definitions, but also for skills. Revisit old problem types every week alongside new ones, so nothing slips away before the exam.

5.

Make it a habit

Ten to twenty minutes of review a day beats hours at the weekend. Attach it to something you already do, such as after breakfast or on the bus, and keep the cards somewhere you will see them.
