Chapter 1

Study Methods That Work

Rereading and highlighting feel productive but do little. These methods feel harder, and that is exactly why they work.

1.

Retrieval practice

Pull information out of your memory instead of putting it back in. Close your notes and write everything you remember, use flashcards, or answer practice questions. The effort of recalling strengthens the memory far more than rereading.

2.

Spaced practice

Spread study across several shorter sessions on different days rather than one long session. A little forgetting between sessions makes each review more effective, and the learning lasts longer.

3.

Interleaving

Mix different types of problems or topics in one session. It feels slower, but it teaches you to choose the right method, not just repeat it.

  • Maths: mix fractions, decimals and percentages.
  • Languages: mix tenses in one practice set.
  • History: mix causes from different periods.
4.

Elaboration and examples

Ask yourself why and how questions about what you are learning, and connect it to things you already know. Find concrete examples for every abstract idea; they give memory something to hold on to.

5.

Dual coding

Combine words with visuals. Draw a diagram, a timeline or a quick sketch next to your notes, then explain the picture in words. Two routes into memory are better than one.
